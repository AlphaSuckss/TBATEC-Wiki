## Keystones

A <span class="thing">keystone</span> is a small cube of matte black stone left by the djinn. Each holds one insight into aether; the djinn remnant called the first a mind's compass. Its insight is won inside it, in a realm of the mind, while the body lies helpless, and there is no leaving until it is won. Arthur solved four: Aroa's Requiem, Realmheart, King's Gambit (from the keystone the phoenix Mordain kept) and an insight into Fate.

## Who May

Here every godrune but the sixth, Spatium, has a keystone: Destruction's and God Step's too (in the books those two came in battle, on the black platform and in the djinn remnant's duel; their keystones hold those fights). **Keystones do nothing before the rebirth**: to anyone not reborn in an asuran body, one is a cold, heavy stone.

Use one standing on solid ground outside the Relictombs: your body lies down where you stand and you wake in its realm.

## Won or Lost

**No waking by choice.** The trial is won or lost. It is lost if you fail its game, if the realm's patience runs out (shown at the top of the screen), if anything harms your <span class="thing">sleeping body</span> (you take the harm), or if you log out. A lost trial throws you back to your body <span class="thing">with the keystone</span>, which then stays closed a while: 2, 5, 10 or 20 minutes by its difficulty.

Won, the keystone crumbles to dust (the first did so in the book) and its godrune is yours.

## The Trials

**God Step (easy)**: the remnant's duel: step where you look, or behind it; strike its back in the breath after; leave the purple flicker before its spear comes through. Three strikes win, five wounds lose<br>**Destruction (medium)**: the black platform: leave the fists' shadows, burn each fist while it rests, then the bowed head. Every burning feeds the Pull (100 loses you); never step into the voice's golden door

**Aroa's Requiem (medium)**: the hall of mirrors: set twelve facets in the frame and turn each till its rune lies true; a Dead Relic carried makes the next right face shimmer<br>**Realmheart (hard)**: the grotto: in the pool, pulse your aether only when a mote crosses the node while the node shines gold (the tide high). Six true pulses; five false ones lose it

**King's Gambit (hard)**: the court of scratches: runes that vanish when looked at, read from the corner of the eye. Join each to its opposite (itself upside down); all five pairs must hold at once<br>**Fate (the hardest)**: the loom: nine rooms, doors that open and shut as it turns. Reach your face on its turn; God Step passes a shut door twice, Requiem turns the loom back three times; a life that ends as you enter it sends you back

## Hardness

Why the difficulties: God Step's duel is a single rule learned once (the flicker, then the step); the platform and the dodecahedron each ask for patience and pacing; Realmheart and the scratches ask for two or more things held in the mind at once, as Arthur's did; the loom asks for a plan over many turns with the other edicts' arts, and few mistakes.

## Fate and Finding

**The Fate keystone** opens only by the Djinn Sanctuary's sleeping gate (Arthur's body lay hidden below the sanctuary) and only to one holding God Step, Aroa's Requiem, King's Gambit and a godrune of life (Destruction or Realmheart): in the book Arthur worked all three edicts and the crown inside it.

**Where they lie**: the Fallen Ascent's black platform and first ruin give Destruction's and God Step's; the ruins and the phoenixes keep the others; deeper vaults and convergences hold them too (God Step's from depth 1, Destruction's and the Aevum keystone from 2, Realmheart's from 3, Mordain's from 4, Fate's only at 5).

## Ellie's Part

In the second keystone Arthur meditated with his sister Ellie beside him, shaping pure mana, and watching it showed him how aether shapes mana.

Here a friend may <span class="thing">sneak-use your sleeping body</span> and spend a tenth of their mana: for twenty seconds the grotto's threads brighten and the motes are easier to meet.
